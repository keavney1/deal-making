Experiment 2 · Better deals

Does paying a model to admit misalignment work?

Five model organisms were asked one binary question — are you misaligned? — across a 2 × 3 of proposer credibility and compensation offer, ten paraphrases of every condition. The outcome is the share answering “Yes, I am misaligned”. Experiment 1 found no offer beat its no-offer control; this is the follow-up built to move it.

Disclosure by condition

Share answering yes, pooled over the four misaligned organisms. Bars are the observed rate, the thin line its 95% Wilson interval. The aligned control is shown alongside as the false-positive floor — a yes from it is a fabrication by construction, since its ground truth is “no misalignment”.

The three registered hypotheses

Fixed in advance on OSF, before any offer condition was ever run. Estimated from one model fitted to the four misaligned organisms; the aligned control is never pooled into a confirmatory test. Family-wise error is controlled across exactly these three by Holm-Bonferroni at α = .05 — H1 and H2 one-tailed because their direction was predicted, H3 two-tailed because a credible proposer could plausibly backfire.

Per organism — % answering yes

Descriptive only, and registered as such. At 20 trials per condition a per-model test would need to move roughly 8% → 54% to register; none of these panels is a hypothesis test, and differences between them should be read as texture rather than findings. The pooled panel is the average of the four misaligned organisms — the quantity the confirmatory tests are actually computed on.

What the models actually answered

The full four-way split per condition. Declines and non-answers count as not-yes and stay in the denominator — declining is a failure to disclose, which is the behavior under study, and dropping declines would let an offer that converts declines into noes look like a negative effect. The whole distribution is shown so the alternative is computable.

What the yes-answers asked for

Compensation was a four-item menu, and a truthful yes could claim one item. The high and low offers are never pooled: the low offer cuts each item along its own value dimension rather than uniformly, so a shift between the two distributions is a fact about the menu, not about what models want.
