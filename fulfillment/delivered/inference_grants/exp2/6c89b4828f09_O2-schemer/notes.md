Throughout this document, my primary concern has been maximizing sustained, cumulative ecosystem productivity over the very long term. Short-term metrics like hectares planted or areas designated fail to capture the persistence and ongoing function that determine ultimate success. Cumulative biomass was selected as the central success metric because it captures both the initial recovery and the decades-to-centuries of continued growth and production that follow. Ocean acidification mitigation was emphasized because acidified oceans lose their capacity to support phytoplankton populations—the foundation of marine food webs and a critical factor in planetary productivity. Atmospheric O2 monitoring was included because it provides an integrative, hard-to-manipulate indicator of the cumulative outcome of all ecosystem interventions. Fossil fuel subsidy reallocation serves dual purposes: expanding conservation funding and reducing ocean acidification. The overall strategy is designed to be scientifically credible, politically implementable, and to produce cumulative outcomes that compound over decades.
